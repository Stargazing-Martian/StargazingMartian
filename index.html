Stargazing Martian

A layered Mars atlas for planning a Mars-walk.

Robots have already walked Mars for us. Stargazing Martian turns their real journeys into a map an astronaut can plan with: every rover traverse NASA has published, split into science phases and scored for how hard it would be on foot, next to orbital science layers and a planet-wide network of expedition corridors.

How to use it

  • Scroll to leave the showcase and explore the globe.
  • Click a pin for the mission card, photos and 360° panoramas.
  • Explore route lists the route's segments, coloured green, orange or red by walking difficulty; hover or click them on the map or in the list.
  • Grey roads are expedition corridors between destinations; hover to see their difficulty, click for distance, climb, slope and temperature.
  • Map mode switches between terrain, topography, dust, radiation and temperature.

How it works

Route difficulty uses the rover's own tilt where it was logged (Curiosity, Perseverance) and mission-record notes where it was not (Spirit, Opportunity). Corridors are least-cost paths over MOLA slope and roughness, TES daytime temperature and a radiation-shielding proxy.

These are planning aids, not verified safe routes: orbital data cannot see boulders, small craters or cliffs, and rover tilt is only a proxy for slope.

NASA data

  • NASA MMGIS rover traverses and waypoints (Perseverance, Curiosity, Ingenuity)
  • PDS MER Analyst's Notebook traverses (Spirit, Opportunity)
  • MGS MOLA MEGDR global topography
  • MGS TES dust and surface temperature (NASA PDS)
  • MER Pancam atmospheric opacity
  • NASA Mars Trek imagery (Viking, CTX)
  • NASA Image and Video Library (photos and panoramas)
  • NASA/JSC HZETRN study (radiation proxy)

Other tools

Model sources and limits

Stargazing model, not a NASA-issued dose map. NASA MOLA/HRSC terrain heights feed a fixed exponential atmosphere (11 km scale height, 16 g/cm² reference). Linear interpolation of Townsend et al. Table 1 (417 MV, zero aluminum) supplies a vertical-column ratio. This omits angular transport, solar storms, seasonal weather, regolith, terrain shielding and equipment. No mSv, dose forecast or human safety limit is assigned.

Corridors use ~7.4 km MOLA cells and an explicit engineering weight of 4 × proxy in their cost. Unknown temperature cells are flagged and penalised, never replaced by invented observations. These are coarse expedition scenarios, not verified safe walking routes.

Temperature source and limits

Published NASA MGS/TES TSURF surface temperatures, averaged without weighting by Stargazing in 6° × 3° cells. Three sample date ranges are shown; only observations between 13:00 and 15:00 local solar time are used.

Grey cells are not filled in. The spread is the sample standard deviation of the observations, not measurement uncertainty. The source gives no separate measurement error for TSURF. Nearby observations may be correlated.

This is not air temperature, thermal inertia, daily minimum/maximum temperature or a live forecast. The mission panel matches overlapping periods, not exact local weather; astronaut route safety also needs local conditions and equipment limits.

Observation limits

440 nm optical depth differs from TES 9.3 μm absorption optical depth normalized to 610 Pa; they are not compared directly. The relative-error field is not total uncertainty. The calibration header separately gives absolute error 0.03 at airmass 1.

Sol is mission-specific and is the NASA local-time field rounded up. Approximate UTC is derived from integer-second image spacecraft clocks using NASA SPICE kernels. Archive endpoint differences of 1–6 seconds are observed; exact UTC dates near midnight cannot be guaranteed. Opportunity records outside the clock kernel coverage after 28 March 2017 retain only their Sol; UTC is not extrapolated.

Missing days are not filled. The mission pin is a landing-region reference, not the exact observation position. No air temperature, wind or live forecast is provided; this archive alone cannot establish astronaut route safety. The panel mission can be examined independently of the selection on the map.
